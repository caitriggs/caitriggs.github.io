Cooperative · Cockpit · 20 minutes

Sky Team

Luc Rémond · Le Scorpion Masqué · Spiel des Jahres award winner, 2024

You fly it. I sit right seat. We land this thing without saying a word.

Small box, tiny footprint, one shared control panel that sits between us and looks like an actual instrument cluster. We are pilot and co-pilot bringing an airliner down, and the whole game lives in that cockpit.

The gorgeous, evil part: dice get rolled behind a screen where the other person cannot see them, and from that moment on the two of you go silent. All that is left is watching where your partner puts a die and working out what they need from you. Most romantic thing a dice game has ever done.

Eleven real airports, twenty one scenarios built from real landing conditions. Wind, ice, kerosene leaks, traffic, and one where you have to train an intern. There is a coffee space on the panel that lets you nudge a die value, which is the single most accurate mechanic in tabletop gaming.

How a round goes

  1. Both of us roll our dice hidden behind our own screens.
  2. We alternate placing dice on the panel: axis, flaps, gear, engines, brakes, radio.
  3. Talking is done for the round. Read what I placed, guess what I am worried about, place accordingly.

Dark fantasy · The Ironlands

Ironsworn

Shawn Tomkin · 2018 · ENnie winner, Best Free Game

Swear a vow out loud, then find out what it costs you.

Cold low fantasy in a land of fjords, glaciers and half abandoned steadings. Early Viking age vibes with the magic dialed way down and made ritual and dangerous. Promises sworn on iron bind you, and your character starts play already owing one.

Co-op mode is our mode. We each play our own character, neither of us gets stuck being the GM, and the world fills itself in through oracle tables we roll against together. What is in the cave, who already lives there, whether the bridge holds. We find out at the same second, which means I get to watch your face do the thing.

It carries across sessions. Vows stay open, scars stay on the sheet, the map keeps growing. Also, and I want to be very clear about this, the entire 272 page rulebook is free.

How a turn goes

  1. Say what your character does.
  2. Roll one d6, add a stat. That number is what you brought.
  3. Roll two d10 against it. Beat both and you get what you wanted. Beat one and you get it with strings attached. Beat neither and the Ironlands take their turn.

Romance · Any setting we want

Star Crossed

Alex Roberts · art by Jess Fink · 2019 Diana Jones Award

Two characters who really want to, and really should not.

We build a pair who are pulled toward each other and have a very good reason to stay apart. Best friends about to move to different cities. A queen and her knight errant. Rival clan leaders. A lonely trucker and an alien brain parasite, which is an actual worked example in the book and tells you everything about its sense of humor.

Then we play scenes, trading off describing what our characters say and do. Any time you want yours to lean into the pull, you pull a brick from the tower and set it on top. The tension becomes a physical object sitting between us getting worse and worse.

If it falls, they act on it. We decide what that looks like and the rules tell us what it costs. Epidiah Ravachol, who designed Dread, said the thing hiding in that teetering tower is real human desire, and he is annoyingly right.

The entire mechanic

  1. Play a scene. Take turns describing what your character does.
  2. Push toward the attraction, pull a brick.
  3. Tower falls, they give in. We narrate it, the rules handle the aftermath.

How to pick

You have twenty minutes and a beer. Sky Team. Set it up on the tailgate, crash the plane twice, land it on the third try, immediately want to go again. Smallest box, lowest commitment, best odds we play it more than once.
You want something that keeps going. Ironsworn. Characters we carry from trip to trip, vows we are still chasing in October, a map of the Ironlands that grows every time we camp. Costs nothing to find out whether it grabs you.
You want one loaded evening with a real ending. Star Crossed. An hour or two, a definite finish, and a tower on the table doing the work of raising the stakes so neither of us has to.
