Sleep at a vineyard!

Two nights parked on the grass between the rows, up on a hillside in Tennessee where the fog settles in the low ground before sunrise. Go in the fall and they will let you pick, so bring your buckets and come home with the fruit.

Where you're going

Tsali Notch Vineyard

A family hillside in the Tennessee Overhill, and the largest muscadine vineyard in the state. Grown without chemicals since 2009.

Two hundred acres of Monroe County farmland, with six thousand vines running twenty-one miles of trellis and the Smokies stacked up blue behind them. The tasting room was built from timber two centuries old, and there is a farmhouse beside it that has been standing since the 1800s. You will be parked in the middle of all of it, with the whole valley going gold in the evening.

The trip  /  two nights at the vineyard

Drive In. Boondock at the grapes.

Mornings come up cool and the fog sits in the low rows until the sun gets around to it. You'll pick until your arms complain, eat lunch in a camp chair with the whole valley laid out in front of you, then wander up for a glass when the afternoon turns gold. Back to the rig for dinner on the grill, and nobody anywhere expecting you.

DAY ONE Weaverville to Tsali Notch Vineyard by way of a cattle ranch in Hayesville

Head west with the whole day in front of you. Around Hayesville, pull off at Walnut Hollow and buy a few steaks from the family who raised them, then carry on along 64 and over the state line. You should be rolling through the vineyard gate by late afternoon. Park between the rows, get the grill going, and let the sun drop behind the ridge while dinner cooks.

Weaverville → Hayesville → Madisonville, TN
About 4.5 hrs driving · farm store open 10 to 4

DAY TWO The Tasting Room and twenty-one miles of trellis

Nothing to pack and nowhere to drive. Go out early while the air is still cool and pick until the bucket is too heavy to be sensible, then walk up to the old log tasting room and work your way through all six of the wines at your own pace. Ring ahead and JD will take you out through the vineyard himself. Everyone who visits ends up writing about him afterwards.

Tasting room: Wed & Thu 12–6, Fri–Sun 10–6
Private tastings and tours by appointment

DAY THREE The Cherohala Skyway the long way home, if the light is good

Get the fruit into the cooler before the heat comes up, and ask about buying in bulk while you settle your bill, since they sell it by the quantity to people doing exactly what you are about to do in your kitchen. Then choose your road. The interstate has you home by supper. The Skyway takes the whole afternoon and gives you forty miles of blue ridgelines for the trouble.

Worth pulling over for

Two more, should you fancy them.

Walnut Hollow RV Ranch

A hundred and fifty-six acres of working cattle ranch outside Hayesville, sitting right on your road west. The family raises their own Black Angus and sells it from the farm store, which is where the first night's dinner comes from. There is a stocked pond for anyone who wants to fish and a trail through the woods for anyone who wants to walk, and near dusk the cows and the deer and the wild turkeys all wander down the hill together. It is a Harvest Hosts stop as well, so if the drive feels long you can simply stay the night and go on in the morning.

HAYESVILLE, NC · (828) 389‑8931

The Cherohala Skyway

Forty miles of high ridgeline between Tellico Plains and Robbinsville, built wide and graded gently for people who came to look at the view rather than fight the road. It may well be the finest drive in the Southeast, and in late September the hills have only just begun to turn. One thing to know: there is genuinely nothing out there, no fuel and no food and no signal for the whole run, so fill the tank before you start and let it have the entire day.

The real reason

Come home with the fruit, not just a bottle.

Every autumn they open the rows and let people pick muscadines for their own juice, their own jelly, their own wine.

No kits here, which suits you better anyway. They're the largest muscadine grower in Tennessee and they'll sell you fruit by the bucket or the bushel, so you can skip the concentrate and start from real grapes for once. After all those boxes, you've earned a proper harvest.

Six varieties on the hillside, two dark and four bronze, and nothing sprayed on any of it.
